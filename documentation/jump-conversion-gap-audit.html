Pokémon Trainer Conversion Gap Audit

What the seven-page, image-only Generation V Pokémon Trainer CYOA demonstrates about converting real Jump documents into interactive packages.

Status: both caller-selected workflows rebuilt and the semantic archive manually re-audited. The semantic archive uses live text and layouts plus artwork-only assets. The facsimile archive preserves measured source panels with additive live rails. Format 1 now supports Jump grants, source maximums, Choice-granted group discounts, Section locking, vertical rules, weighted tracks, growth, spans, bounded geometry and typography, hard breaks, list presentation, and compact interaction slots; the remaining entries below are demonstrated gaps, not missing attempts.

Source and authority

  • Title: Pokémon Trainer CYOA.
  • Visible author and edition: Quicksilver, Gen V.
  • Physical source: seven PDF pages, each containing one 1920×2160 raster image and no text layer.
  • Authoritative wording: the developer-supplied PDF. Web material is corroboration only, including a contemporary build narrative and a secondary flaw transcription.
  • Visual anchors: exact #00ffff cyan and #404040 charcoal, square cards, condensed display type, dark rules, map crops, starter art, and item sprites.

Two rejected extremes and the corrective method

The initial Kanto rendering used a conventional vertical card: a small heading, centered map, prose below, detached cost badge, control at the bottom, and substantial empty cyan space. The source is a dense horizontal strip with a place-name lockup at left, prose in the middle, and its map flush right. Archive validity and content presence were incorrectly treated as completion evidence.

The next pass reproduced nearly every panel as a source image. That proved a useful facsimile technique but skipped expressible authoring work: the Region introduction can be a Stack of headings and horizontal rules followed by an Inline containing the Roll/Pay Stack and world text; a Region card can be an Inline of its live identity lockup, description, and map. The same mistake affected Skills, Gear, Flaws, Future, and the Starting Kit strip.

The corrected semantic workflow performs that decomposition first. The Region header and location tiles use live Text, horizontal and vertical Rule, Stack, Inline, cost, and control slots; only irreducible artwork remains image content. Starting Kit artwork is split into six sprite crops with live labels. All control and roll slots make an explicit adornment decision. The separately built facsimile archive remains an ordinary archive selected by the caller's conversion request; it is not an application profile or replacement for semantic authoring.

The persistent process correction is defined in the visual-fidelity protocol: measure, conduct a semantic expressibility audit, compare at equal width, list the five largest differences, and cite exact missing primitives for anything blocked.

Manual semantic re-audit

The August 2026 passes inventoried every visible source block before classifying a mismatch. They rejected source-facing converter commentary, rasterized masthead text, paraphrased bullet lists, accidental crop-edge rules, a muted-text renderer override, equal Grid cells with shorter visible cards, and equal outer Inline height with incorrect nested Stack distribution as preventable defects. The latest pass preserved verified artwork and transcription while changing only the layouts implicated by demonstrated gaps.

Represented capabilities

  • Ordinary flat costs, negative-cost drawback awards, and repeatable per-purchase costs.
  • Manual, random, and either resolution with rolled results becoming free.
  • Single and multi sources, direct Choice placements, text, select, and bounded integer controls.
  • Properties for Age, Gender, Origin, Region, and starter tier.
  • Durable perks, items, a companion, companion-owned perks, and Jump-local traits.
  • Inherited and grant-specific tags using the existing searchable vocabulary.
  • Artwork-only logo, sprite, map, and starter crops with live surrounding labels and prose.
  • Reusable semantic layouts, exact palette colors, two- and three-column direct-choice grids, source-order card anatomy, and live dynamic cost badges.
  • Control and roll slots with decorative adornments disabled.
  • Unconditional Jump-level grants for durable inventory, companions, forms, resources, properties, and current-Jump traits.
  • Optional multi-source maximums without implicit minimums, requirements, or defaults.
  • Origin-granted flat and percentage discounts targeted only through Choice groups, with live base/resolved Cost provenance.
  • Initially locked Sections and Choice-granted signed lock/unlock effects.
  • Vertical rules that stretch to the shared Inline row extent.
  • Weighted Grid tracks, row/column spans, and direct Stack/Inline growth with responsive single-column collapse.
  • Axis padding, bounded minimum dimensions and aspect ratios, stronger text tokens or exact bounded text sizes, approved system typography, hard breaks, semantic list styling, and compact Control/Cost slots.

Closed: conditional economics

Observed source behavior: Identities make named Skills free or half price, and Moneybags makes several Gear entries free or discounted.

Supported mapping: each Origin Choice grants flat or percentage discounts to the semantic groups carried by affected Skills or Gear. Package policy chooses strongest-only or stacked evaluation and zero or negative floors. Cost badges retain base price, resolved price, and granting-Choice provenance.

GAP-002: constraints, dependencies, and overrides

Observed source behavior: no more than two Flaws; Flaws override incompatible Skills and Identities; Legendaries are forbidden; some equipment and abilities refer to other selections.

Current loss: the source-authored two-Flaw maximum is now expressible, but ordinary Choices still cannot declare general requirements, conflicts, suppression, species legality, or override predicates. Form dependency is a narrow ownership feature, not a general solution.

Reference fallback: use max: 2 for Flaws and preserve only the remaining override and legality rules in prose and the ledger.

Future capability: typed requires, conflicts, and overrides relationships with explicit invalid-state policy rather than arbitrary source order.

GAP-003: required and default state

Observed source behavior: the starting kit is given automatically; every build needs a Region, Identity, starter, and final outcome; rolled Age and previous Gender provide derived defaults.

Current loss: choices can always return to unset and groups are not required. This permissiveness is intentional; source maximums do not imply a minimum or default.

Supported mapping: the Starting Kit and Jump Terms are Jump grants and activate unconditionally. Age roll and Gender continuity use existing derived behavior. Region, Origin, and starter tier remain optional single sources.

Future capability: only if justified by broader author needs, explicit completion constraints distinguishable from defaults and automatic grants. They must not force every Jump into an exactly-one design.

GAP-004: first-class options and linked configuration

Observed source behavior: a rolled Free Pick unlocks a second Region choice; starter species, availability tier, hidden ability, and upgrades configure one companion; repeated Gear names fixed-size batches.

Current loss: Select options are strings without handles, images, tags, costs, grants, or dynamic grant targets. A single property can bridge some state, but it cannot express option-specific mechanics or validate a linked configuration.

Reference fallback: use a Select Choice inside Free Pick; create the starter companion from a separate text control; represent tiers and upgrades as sibling Choices; measure Gear by purchase count rather than physical unit count.

Future capability: structured options or another linked-configuration concept with stable identities, per-option presentation and mechanics, typed output, and bounded dynamic grant targeting. Avoid turning option blocks into an unrestricted scripting language.

GAP-005: Jump lifecycle and retention outcomes

Observed source behavior: Homeward Bound, Not Going Back, and The Next Adventure change chain continuation, world residence, time, and which Pokémon or possessions remain.

Current loss: a package describes choices inside a chain entry but cannot terminate, continue, or rewrite the chain, and has no retention-limit engine.

Reference fallback: reproduce Home, Stay, and Next Jump as source prose and visuals only. Do not create controls with no application-owned completion path.

Future capability: an application-owned completion workflow that interprets standardized outcome records after explicit user confirmation. Packages must not receive arbitrary mutation authority over chains.

GAP-006: document-level visual fidelity

Observed source behavior: seven fixed portrait canvases use a condensed display typeface, extreme size contrast, exact line metrics, asymmetrical boxes, fixed art anchors, spanning cards, page-specific two- and three-column compositions, and controls implied by printed instructions rather than inserted into every card.

Current semantic result: the Region header, location strips, Identities, starter tiers, upgrades, Skills, Gear, Flaws, and Future prose are declarative layouts with live content. A separate facsimile archive preserves exact source pixels when the caller requests that workflow.

Facsimile defects that are not format gaps

The tightened facsimile exposed preventable conversion defects. Starting Kit now uses unconditional Jump grants and has no claim checkbox. The one locally centered transition control follows the centered source axis without globally changing alignment. Every Control and Roll slot explicitly disables adornments. The starter species is interpolated into the companion's visible Tracker name. Skill, upgrade, item, and flaw action rails expose live controls, meaningful User-profile Tags, and dynamic Cost badges. Gear crops exclude neighboring gutters and rules. Future is source prose and artwork with no controls. Authored Trait cards inherit their cyan-on-charcoal design without a default white wrapper.

Tag styling is neither a source-fidelity target nor a format gap: the User Tag profile always wins. Centering, separator suppression, dynamic naming, metadata slots, clean cropping, vertical rules, Jump grants, direct Choice association, Trait card styling, and omission of unsupported Future controls must not be reported as blocked.

Semantic evidence standard

The earlier aspect-ratio-only archetype report was retired because it could label a card acceptable while its typography, crop edges, omitted text, internal proportions, or contrast were visibly wrong. Current evidence combines equal-width screenshots, a source-node inventory, crop-edge pixel checks, computed foreground/background contrast, exact casing and content assertions, and interactive-path verification. Aspect ratio remains one measurement, never the verdict.

The later semantic review exposed a second class of preventable defects: overflowing warning text, inverted Identity color and rule order, merged Age and Gender panels, generic Cost corners, inconsistent neighboring control grammar, control-only starter rails, loose Gear lists, colored Flaw control patches, and maximum-selection status changing action widths. Except for the last renderer defect, these were authoring and review failures, not missing syntax. The renderer now keeps repeated at-limit status accessible without injecting it into authored action geometry. It also collapses responsive Grids from the rendered Jump's container width, so a narrow Editor preview no longer stays squeezed merely because the application window is wide.

Future conversion evidence must include a relationship ledger for each archetype and screenshots of interacted states. Cost and Control anchors are reviewed with the source composition, not appended after content. A seven-question adversarial glance gate explicitly rejects clipping, color or rule inversion, merged panels, orphan rails, mismatched neighboring controls, poor internal rhythm, and state-driven geometry changes. Rule checks measure painted adjacency and thickness, not merely DOM order: a parent gap that turns a thin separator into a broad color band is a failure. Repeated Starter, Skill, and Gear cards require group captures and deterministic gutter-color samples, while their text roles require computed typography checks against the source ledger. Passing outer bounds, isolated-card captures, or content-presence assertions cannot override an obvious visual failure.

Closed visual items: proportional integer tracks, spans, direct-child growth, axis padding, bounded minimums and aspect ratios, stronger or exact text sizing, approved system typography controls, explicit hard breaks, dash/list spacing, compact interaction slots, and Grid stretch propagation are supported. A remaining mismatch in those areas is an authoring defect until a new minimal valid experiment proves otherwise.

Conclusion: substantially more of the source is semantically expressible than either rejected pass demonstrated. Exact unavailable source-font identity, fixed page geometry, authored breakpoint variants, and bounded overlays remain blocked; the composition itself must still be attempted with live nodes.

Future capability: evaluate safe packaged fonts with rights metadata, named control zones or bounded overlays, breakpoint variants, and optional document/page modes. These features should remain declarative and must not introduce arbitrary CSS or script execution.

GAP-007: source and conversion provenance

Observed need: the package needs to distinguish Quicksilver's authorship and Gen V source edition from the later conversion, extraction method, authority file, and rights status.

Current loss: author, version, and free-form description cannot represent source edition, converter, source URL or fingerprint, license, extracted-asset provenance, or fidelity status without conflating concepts.

Reference fallback: retain the original author, use an explicit conversion-attempt version suffix, place process data in the ignored ledger, and keep copyrighted source assets out of the repository.

Future capability: bounded package metadata for source identity, edition, converter/tool, source fingerprint, rights or license note, and conversion timestamp. None of it should weaken archive validation or expose the source PDF to rendering.

Priority for future design

  1. Dependencies and overrides: source maximums and conditional economics are closed; general requirements, conflicts, and override behavior remain.
  2. Completion policy: consider required/default state only as an author-selected capability, never an implicit exactly-one rule.
  3. First-class options and linked targets: common in configurable companions, forms, items, and tiered purchases.
  4. Lifecycle outcomes: important but should be application-mediated and security-conscious.
  5. Provenance metadata: important for a sustainable conversion library and rights review.
  6. Control zones and overlays: bounded normal-flow escape hatches remain the largest barrier to integrating interaction with sources that truly overlap content.
  7. Packaged typography and optional page modes: pursue rights-aware fonts and bounded page behavior that improve interactive documents broadly and preserve package security.

Acceptance boundary

The semantic and facsimile archives are separate caller-selected outputs and both pass package validation. The semantic archive remains visually blocked on exact unavailable source-font identity, fixed pagination or authored breakpoint variants, and bounded overlays. Weighted tracks, spans, rich-text markers, hard breaks, and compact interaction are no longer gaps. The facsimile archive supplies exact pixels with known accessibility and dynamic-state losses. Neither workflow may be called complete without its own explicit evidence and limitations.
